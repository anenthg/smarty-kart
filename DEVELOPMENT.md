# Neon Racer

An AirConsole-style racing game. The TV shows the race and the phones are the controllers. The four cars, the pickups, the city track, the textures and the loading-screen render are all generated by Blender scripts.

## Play

```bash
cd ~/Projects/kid-racer
npm start
```

1. **TV:** open `http://<mac-ip>:8080` in the TV's browser (the "My Game" tile; the address is printed in the terminal). On this Mac it's `http://localhost:8080`.
2. **Phones:** connect to the same Wi-Fi and scan the QR code on the TV. Type a name and tap JOIN.
3. **Garage:** pick a car and paint it (see below). The TV lobby shows everyone's cars live.
4. Tap **START RACE** on any phone. The race is 3 laps (add `?laps=1` to the TV URL for a quick race).
5. Press **OK** on the remote once (or click) to turn on sound. Browsers need one press before they play audio; that first press only turns the sound on.

### Garage (phone lobby)

- **Cars**, chosen with ◀ ▶ on the spinning 3D preview (drag it to turn it):

  | Car | Good at | Speed / Zoom / Grip |
  |---|---|---|
  | Viper | all-rounder | 3 / 3 / 3 |
  | Bolt | top speed | 5 / 2 / 3 |
  | Tank | acceleration, wins bumps (heavier) | 3 / 5 / 2 |
  | Zip | twisty turns | 2 / 4 / 5 |

- **Paint** (12 colours), **Style** (plain, stripes, flames, lightning, checkers, stars, plus a style colour), **Shine** (shiny, metallic, matte, chrome, rainbow), **Glow** (underglow colour or off).
- Choices are saved on the phone and come back next time.
- After a race, **🎨 Change car** returns everyone to the garage.

### Racing moves (Mario Kart style)

- **Drift → mini-turbo (automatic):** push the stick hard into a corner at speed and the car slides. Sparks charge blue → orange → purple; ease off the stick to release a short, medium or long boost.
- **Rocket start:** watch the start lights over the line. Press GO when the 3rd red light comes on ("1"), not before, for a boost off the line. Holding GO earlier just starts normally (and Auto-gas never gets it).
- **Slipstream:** drive close behind another car for a second and you get a burst of speed (wind lines show it charging).
- **Coins:** each coin adds a little top speed, up to 10. Getting hit drops 3.
- **Spin-outs:** bananas, orbs, horn blasts and cars with a star or rocket spin you around for a moment; then you flicker and are safe for 2 seconds.
- **Music** plays during the race and speeds up on the final lap (add `?music=0` to the TV address to turn it off).

### On the track

- **🪙 Coins** in wiggly rows: each one adds a little boost, and coins are counted on the results screen.
- **? Item boxes** in rows of four: drive through one for a random item. It fires by itself a moment later:
  - 🍌 **Banana** (×1 or ×3): dropped behind you; whoever drives over it spins.
  - 🟢 **Bouncy orb**: shoots straight ahead and bounces off walls.
  - 🔴 **Homing orb**: chases the car right in front of you.
  - ⚡ **Boost** (×1 or ×3): a quick burst of speed each.
  - 🪙 **+3 coins** and 📯 **Horn blast** (spins cars close by and clears bananas and orbs around you).
  - 🚀 **Rocket**: drives itself through traffic for about 2 seconds and knocks cars aside.
  - ⭐ **Star**: 6 seconds of extra speed, rainbow car; walls don't slow you and cars you touch spin out.
  - 🧲 **Magnet**: 8 seconds of pulling in coins from far away.
  - Like Mario Kart, the leader mostly gets bananas, coins and horns; cars further back get rockets, stars, homing orbs and boosts.
- **⚡ Speed pads** (glowing chevrons): free boost when you drive over one.

### Phone controls and feedback

**Phone controls:** the left half is a big thumbstick that only moves left and right, Nintendo style. A small push turns gently, a full push turns hard, and it springs back to the middle when you let go. The right half has **GO** on top and **REVERSE** below; REVERSE brakes first, then backs up. Items fire by themselves a moment after you get one, and boost fires by itself when the bar is full. For small kids, tick **Auto-gas** so they only need to steer.

**Tilt steering (optional):** tick **Tilt steer** in the garage, then hold the phone sideways and turn it like a steering wheel. The thumbstick area then shows the wheel; tap it to re-center.

**⏸ Pause** is in the phone's top bar during a race. It freezes the TV and shows **▶ RESUME** (restarts with a 3-2-1) or **🏠 Quit race** (back to the garage) on every phone. On the TV keyboard, P pauses and OK/Enter resumes.

During a race the phone shows position, lap and coins. It reacts to every event with vibration (Android), short sounds (switch off with **Sounds**), screen flashes and pop-up messages: countdown, GO, coin, speed pad, item roulette, item ready, item used, boost ready, bumps (screen shake), new lap, final lap, wrong way and the finish. Brushing or hitting the side walls gives a light buzz, and bumping another car a stronger one. iPhones can't vibrate from a web page, so the sounds and flashes do that job there.

Motion sensors for tilt only work on the `https://…:8443/play` link (the QR code uses it). The certificate is self-signed, so the first visit shows a "not private" warning: on iPhone tap *Show Details → visit this website*, on Android *Advanced → Proceed*. iPhones then ask for motion access; tap Allow. The plain `http://…:8080/play` link works with buttons only.

Pinch-zoom and double-tap zoom are blocked on the controller page.

Up to 4 players. With **2 players the TV splits left/right**, each with their own chase camera. With 3–4 players everyone shares one camera that frames all the human cars, and anyone who drops more than ~70 m behind gets a 🌀 catch-up warp to the pack. If the TV can't keep split screen above ~28 fps, it switches to the shared camera by itself ("Shared screen for smoother racing") until the page is reloaded. Each phone shows its own position, lap and item. A phone that locks or leaves goes on autopilot. Bots with random cars and paint jobs fill the grid. Kid-friendly helpers: steering assist, soft walls, automatic reset if you get stuck or go the wrong way, and bots that stay close to the players.

No phone? Remote D-pad or arrow keys to drive (pressing one joins a "Remote" player), Space for boost, Z for item, OK/Enter to start.

## Running on the TV (Mali-450, WebGL 1)

The TV's GPU only does WebGL 1, so the game is built for it:

- three.js is pinned to **0.162.0**, the last release that supports WebGL 1. Don't upgrade it.
- One render pass at 960×540, with no post-processing. Glow is additive geometry, car paint uses matcaps, the track is unlit with baked vertex-colour lighting, and fog is linear.
- No `InstancedMesh`, no 32-bit indices, no float textures, no PBR materials. Coins, item boxes, pads, shadows and glows are each one merged draw call.
- HUD and minimap update at 10 Hz. If the frame rate stays under target, the render resolution steps down by itself.

Debugging the TV, which has no DevTools:

- `http://<mac-ip>:8080/?debug=1` shows an fps, draw-call and triangle counter on screen. It also sends errors, console output and stats every 5 s to the Mac terminal running `npm start` (the `POST /__log` endpoint).
- `http://localhost:8080/?emulate=tv&debug=1` on the Mac turns off the WebGL features the TV lacks (WebGL 2, instancing, uint32 indices, float textures, fragment highp). Use it to catch problems before trying the TV.
- Other URL options: `?res=720` to try a different render height, and `?laps=N`.

## Sunny Kart (second game, Mario Kart style)

`http://<mac-ip>:8080` now opens a **Choose a game** start screen (`public/menu.html`): ◀ ▶ and OK on the remote, or tap a game on a
connected phone. Direct links: `/neon` (Neon Racer, unchanged apart from Back → start screen) and `/sunny`. Same server, phones and QR code;
phones stay connected when the game changes, and the TV's `state` message tells the controller which layout to show (`game: 'menu' | 'sunny'`).

Phone in Sunny Kart: racer picker (Ruby / Lime / Sunny / Plum, no two players on the same racer), 🎮 Games button back to the start screen,
and race controls: stick | ITEM (tap) · REVERSE / HOP-DRIFT (hold) · GO. Holding HOP/DRIFT also accelerates.

- Track: Mario Kart 3.js's "SNES Mario Circuit 3" model (a fan rip of Nintendo's track; private family use only, don't share it).
  `blender/sunny/build_sunny_track.py` welds the triangle soup, decimates it from 460k to ~35k triangles, turns the rounded
  barrier blocks into boxes, bakes sunlight into vertex colours, and measures the road (centre line, width, walls) into `track.json`.
- Racers and kart parts: `blender/sunny/build_karts.py` builds 4 drivers (Ruby, Lime, Sunny, Plum), 4 bodies (Classic, Buggy, Rocket,
  Bubble: a white 'Paint' mesh tinted in game + a 'Trim' mesh) and 4 wheel sets (Standard, Big, Slick, Roller; `kart_parts.json` has the
  lift per set). `racers.js` holds the stat changes per part (`kartStats` / `kartPhys`). Phone: Racer / Kart / Wheels / Colour tabs with a
  live 3D preview (`public/sunny/preview.js`). Check all 16 combinations in `blender/sunny/parts_sheet.png`.
- Character feel: a 4.6 s intro before the countdown (flyover, then along the grid, racers shout; OK skips; `?intro=0` turns it off),
  drivers bounce after a purple turbo or a hit on someone and wobble with dizzy stars after a spin, and a 1-2-3 podium with confetti
  and the winner's voice line after the race ("Great try!" if your kid isn't in the top 3).
- Handling ported from Mario Kart 3.js: hop + drift (Space on the keyboard; hold it with the stick into a corner), blue/orange/purple
  sparks, mini-turbo on release, sand slows you, walls bounce you back. Items: banana, green/red shell, mushroom, star, bullet, coins, horn.
- Keyboard/remote: arrows drive, Space = hop/drift, Z = item (the remote fires items by itself), Back in the lobby = game menu (once it exists).
- Sound: ElevenLabs sound effects, character voices (Ruby: Adik, Lime: Omar, Sunny: Chutki, Plum: Piku; announcer: Charlie) and two
  music loops in `public/sunny/audio/` (2.2 MB, 83 clips). `python3 tools/gen_sunny_audio.py` generates only missing clips (raw API output
  cached in `tools/audio_raw/`, so re-processing is free); `--redo name1,name2` regenerates. Missing files fall back to the synth sounds.
- Rebuild: `blender -b --factory-startup -P blender/sunny/build_sunny_track.py` and `... build_karts.py` (source GLBs in `blender/sunny/src/`).

## Rebuild the 3D assets (Blender 5.x)

```bash
npm run assets      # matcaps, cars, pickups and track, headless
```

- `blender/build_matcaps.py`: renders the lit-sphere matcaps (`matcap_gloss/metal/matte/chrome/pearl/detail/gold.png`) that give the cars their shine with one texture read.
- `blender/build_car.py`: the four cars from one set of profile parameters (`SPECS`). Each car is one mesh of about 2,700 triangles with 3 materials (Paint with baked AO, Detail, Wheel). Exports `public/assets/car_<id>.glb`, writes `blender/preview_<id>.png`, and renders `hero.jpg` (`-- --no-hero` skips that; `-- --only=tank` builds one car).
- `blender/build_pickups.py`: coin, item box and boost pad, exported to `pickups.glb`.
- `blender/build_track.py`: the 1.75 km night-city circuit, about 22k triangles. Lighting (lamp pools, neon spill, ambient) is baked into vertex colours, neon glow is additive halo geometry, and everything is merged per material into chunks for culling. Exports `track.glb` and `track.json` (the centre line used for physics). Edit `CTRL` to change the layout.
- `node tools/glbinfo.mjs public/assets/*.glb`: triangles, draw calls, max vertices per primitive and index size, to keep assets inside the TV's limits.
- The old high-detail versions (WebGL 2, bloom, PBR) are kept in `old/`.

## Files

- `server/index.js`: static server (http 8080 + https 8443) and WebSocket relay between the TV and the phones. It remembers each phone's car so a reloaded TV gets it back.
- `public/js/boot.js`: shows startup errors on screen, `?debug=1` reporter, `?emulate=tv`
- `public/js/game.js`, `public/tv.html`, `public/css/tv.css`: the game (three.js r162 on WebGL 1, shared camera, physics, pickups and items, AI, HUD, sound)
- `public/js/garage-data.js`: shared car stats, paint options and the paint/pattern painter (used by TV and phone)
- `public/controller.html`, `public/js/controller.js`, `public/css/controller.css`: phone gamepad, garage and feedback
- `public/js/garage.js`: the phone's 3D car preview


## After-race quiz (both games)

After every finished race, each player answers **5 general-knowledge questions** before the next race can start. PLAY AGAIN, the remote's OK, Back and game switching all stay locked until every player has attempted all five.

- **Questions:** `public/quiz/questions.json`, built from the owner's *SKGKO Class 3 – 100 Question Practice Set* (with the answer key and the paper's explanations). Each quiz takes one question from each of five different sections, with the answer order shuffled. No question repeats until all 100 have been asked; the list of asked questions is in `localStorage` `kr_quiz_seen` on the TV.
- **TV** (`public/quiz/quiz.js` + `quiz.css`, shared by both games): the question card, plus the four answers as giant item boxes on the track (▲◆●■ in red, blue, yellow and green). When everyone has answered, each kart drives into its chosen box.
  - **Right:** the box bursts into confetti, the kid gets a ⭐ and the racer cheers.
  - **Wrong:** a gentle spin-out, the right box glows, and the announcer says "The answer is …". The paper's explanation is shown where it has one.
- **Phone:** four big buttons in the same colours and shapes. No timer.
- **TV remote player:** ◀ ▶ to choose a box, OK to answer.
- **Reward:** each ⭐ = one bonus coin at the start of the next race (Neon Racer also gets +10% boost bar per ⭐).
- **Voice:** the announcer (ElevenLabs, Charlie) reads every question, plus "The answer is …" and a few reactions. `python3 tools/gen_quiz_audio.py` generates missing clips (`--list` to preview): 208 clips, 5.4 MB, ~3,400 credits.
- **To change the questions:** edit `questions.json` (fields `n, q, o[4], a, sec, why`), then run `gen_quiz_audio.py` for the new lines.
- **Protocol:** one new pad→TV message, `{t:'answer', k:0-3}`. During the quiz the state message carries `phase:'quiz'` with `qstate: intro|ask|locked|reveal|done`.


## Sunny Kart karts, version 2 (baked look)

`blender -b --factory-startup -P blender/sunny/build_karts_v2.py -- --out public/sunny/assets/v2` (≈30 s) builds every part into `public/sunny/assets/v2/`. Add `--only classic,ruby,standard` to build a few parts for a quick look, but **finish with a full run**: a partial run writes a `kart_parts.json` that lists only those parts.

- **Bodies** (`body_<id>.glb`, ~2.9–3.3k tris): lofted rounded shapes. One mesh with three materials (Paint / Trim / Chrome) shares one 512² texture baked in Cycles: ambient occlusion × vertex colours, plus details painted from baked position/normal maps (nose stripe, gold-star roundels via `roundel()`).
- **Drivers** (`driver_<racer>.glb`, ~2.9k tris): helmet with a stripe and the racer's letter on the back and sides, face with eyes, cheeks and smile, gloves, chest badge, scarf. The letters come from `blender/sunny/tex2/glyph_*.npy`, pre-rendered with the system Python + PIL (Blender's Python has no PIL).
- **Wheels** (`wheels_<set>.glb`, 864 tris): rounded tyres with tread and rim textures. `Wheel_FL` and `Wheel_FR` steer and spin; `Wheel_R` (the rear pair) spins.
- **Steering wheel** (`steer.glb`): turns with the steering.
- **In game** (`public/sunny/kartlook.js`, shared by `game.js`, `preview.js` and `kartlab.html`): `KartModel` with matcaps drawn in code (candy paint, satin, chrome, skin, rubber), wheel steer/spin, steering wheel and a suspension spring (`bump()` on hop landings and wall hits). `?outline=1` adds a cartoon outline (inverted hull); it is off by default because of the cost.
- **TV cost**, measured with `?emulate=tv` on the Mac (all four karts at the start line): 57 draw calls and ~67k tris at 55–60 fps (before: ~45k tris). With the outline: 69 calls and ~91k tris. Not yet measured on the real TV.
- **Kart lab:** `/sunny/kartlab.html`. Old vs new side by side; `?view=chase|front|side|back`, `&outline=1`, `&single=new`, `&sheet=1` (all 16 body × wheel combinations), `&sheet=backs`.
- Confirmed smooth on the family TV (owner, Nov 18 2026). The version-1 files (`build_karts.py`, the old body/wheels/driver GLBs and `kart_parts.json`) were moved to `archive/sunny_karts_v1/`. The phone's racer pictures (`assets/racer_*.jpg`) were re-captured from the kart lab.


## Difficulty levels (both games)

`public/js/levels.js` holds the three levels: 🐢 Easy (50cc), 🐰 Normal (100cc) and 🔥 Hard (150cc). Normal = how the games played before levels existed.

- **Choosing a level:** one level per race, set in the lobby or on the results screen with the TV remote's Left/Right, or from any phone's LEVEL row (garage, and the results overlay before PLAY AGAIN). It can't change during a race or the quiz.
- **Remembering it:** the TV stores the level in `localStorage` `kr_level`.
- **TV remote:** Left/Right now change the level in the lobby. Up/Down add the remote player.
- **What a level changes:**
  - **Computer racers:** speed multipliers by distance to the leading kid, drift chance (Sunny Kart), and how eagerly they use items. On Easy they never get red shells or homing orbs.
  - **Help for the kid:** steering help strength, wall speed loss (no stun on Easy), sand slowdown (Sunny Kart), and better item odds on Easy.
  - **The kid's own kart handles the same on every level.**
- **Protocol:** pad→TV `{t:'level', v}`; the TV's `state` message carries `level`.
- **Measured** (Sunny Kart, Mac emulation): the same scripted driver for 30 s left the computer racers 32–99 m behind on Easy, roughly level on Normal (+18 to −47 m) and 23–56 m ahead on Hard.


## Neon Racer: 4 lanes, coin count

- **Wider road:** the road went from 3 lanes (16 m) to 4 lanes (21.3 m). `blender/build_track.py` now has `LANE_W` and `N_LANES`; the curbs, walls and verge keep their widths. The centre line is unchanged (nearest separate sections are 70 m apart; the tightest radius is 21 m).
  - `game.js` scales item boxes, boost pads, coin lines, the starting grid, bot lanes, respawn and camera offsets by `track.roadHalf / 8`, so the old track would still work.
  - The 3-lane track is in `archive/track_3lane.*`.
- **Coins:** the count now keeps going past 10. The speed bonus still stops at 10 coins (+1.2% each, like Mario Kart), and a spin still drops 3. Sunny Kart keeps the Mario Kart cap of 10.


## Hosting for friends (Railway, closed beta)

The same `server/index.js` runs at home and on Railway.

- **Rooms:** every TV screen makes up a 4-character room code once (`public/js/room.js`, stored in `localStorage` `kr_room`) and shows it under the QR. The QR link is `/play?room=CODE&k=INVITE`, so phones join that TV only. Rooms hold one TV and up to 4 phones; at most 300 rooms.
- **Hosted mode** switches on when `RAILWAY_ENVIRONMENT` is set (Railway sets it) or `HOSTED=1`:
  - one port (`PORT`) and no self-signed certificate; Railway provides HTTPS;
  - the join link uses the public address;
  - phones without a room code are asked for it;
  - request logging and the `?debug=1` log endpoint are off (turn them on with `LOG_REQUESTS=1` / `DEBUG_LOG=1`).
- **Invite code:** set the `BETA_CODE` variable. Each device enters it once, by typing it into the small form or opening a link with `?k=CODE`, and gets a one-year cookie. The QR on the TV includes the code. Without `BETA_CODE` the site is open to anyone with the address.
- **At home** nothing changes: phones with an old link (no room) join the TV that connected last.
- **Deploy files:**
  - `railway.json`: start command and health check `/health`.
  - `.railwayignore` / `.gitignore`: Blender sources, tools, raw audio, archives, docs and the kart lab are left out, so the upload is ~16 MB.
  - `package.json` `engines` node ≥ 20.
- **Deploy (one of):**
  1. **CLI:** `railway login`, `railway init`, `railway up` in this folder.
  2. **GitHub:** push to a **private** GitHub repo, then in Railway choose *New project → Deploy from GitHub repo*.
- **Then in Railway:**
  - *Variables* → `BETA_CODE=<your code>`;
  - *Settings → Networking* → **Generate Domain**;
  - *Settings → Region* → Southeast Asia (closest to India).
- **For a friend:**
  1. On the TV browser, open `https://<domain>/?k=<code>` once. The TV then remembers the code.
  2. Pick a game; the kids scan the QR with their phones.
- Sunny Kart's track is a fan rip of a Nintendo track. It is included in this closed beta at the owner's decision; keep the repo private and the invite code on.
